\newcommand{\stixSota}{GLIM~\cite{koide2024glim} is a tightly coupled LiDAR--inertial SLAM system whose scan-matching factors (voxelised GICP) are evaluated on the GPU. Its odometry matches each scan against spatially distributed keyframes in a fixed-lag smoother with IMU preintegration, which bridges a few seconds of degenerate range data; submaps are refined by batch optimisation and aligned wherever they overlap, which closes loops without explicit place recognition.}
\newcommand{\stixMethods}{GLIM (the authors' Docker image, glim \texttt{2262aaf}, GPU variant, RTX 3050 Ti laptop GPU) gets the MID-360 point clouds with per-point times and the MID-360 IMU and runs with default parameters; only the topics, the IMU acceleration scale (the Livox IMU reports $g$) and the LiDAR--IMU extrinsic from M3DGR are set. Its IMU-frame trajectory is aligned with SE(3).}
\newcommand{\stixResults}{GLIM, the LiDAR--inertial control, reaches 0.14, 0.20 and 0.14\,m ATE on D, S and V (five runs within 0.1\,mm) and a map F-score of 0.92, 0.96 and 0.93; its exported map is thinned to about 15\,000 points, which lowers completeness, not accuracy (median 1\,cm). Most of this error is the unknown offset between the motion-capture marker and GLIM's IMU frame: a constant offset fitted on the ground truth is 0.20\,m on all three sequences (within 4\,mm) and leaves less than 1\,cm; as it uses the ground truth, this is a diagnostic, not a result. On V, GLIM's error is 0.12\,m in lit and 0.15\,m in dark frames, a difference the same offset explains (4.7 and 3.9\,mm after removing it), and its log reports no failure: the room stays trackable in the dark, so the failures of the camera systems come from the images, not from the scene.}
