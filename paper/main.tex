\subsection{Dataset}
M3DGR~\cite{zhang2025m3dgr} is recorded with a wheeled ground robot carrying an RGB-D camera (RealSense D435i, $640\times480$ at 30\,Hz), two Livox LiDARs (MID-360, Avia), IMUs and wheel odometry; ground truth comes from an OptiTrack motion-capture system at about 300\,Hz. All systems use the same three indoor sequences in one $6.5\times3$\,m room: \emph{Dynamic01} (175\,s, 71\,m, people walking through the scene), \emph{Sha-turn01} (139\,s, 98\,m, sharp turns) and \emph{Varying-illu01} (154\,s, 72\,m, light switched on and off, about 45\,\% of the frames near-black, dark phases up to 10\,s). The first two stand for normal operation, the third tests how the systems cope with darkness.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
We report the absolute trajectory error (ATE)~\cite{sturm2012benchmark}, computed with evo~\cite{grupp2017evo}.
Trajectories are aligned with the Umeyama method~\cite{umeyama1991least}: SE(3) for metric systems and Sim(3) for monocular systems, whose scale is unobservable~\cite{zhang2018tutorial}.
Each system runs five times per sequence; we report the median ATE RMSE with its interquartile range (IQR), the success rate and the tracked fraction, i.e.\ the part of the ground-truth time covered by the estimate~\cite{campos2021orbslam3}.
Maps are compared with a pseudo-ground-truth map built by stacking the MID-360 scans along the motion-capture poses. The offset between the motion-capture marker body and the LiDAR is chosen so that the stacked cloud is as sharp as possible~\cite{levinson2010unsupervised}. Points that are only seen for a short time, mostly people, are removed. After aligning the trajectory and refining with ICP, we report accuracy (estimate to reference), completeness (reference to estimate) and the F-score at 10\,cm~\cite{knapitsch2017tanks}, restricted to the volume observed by both maps.

On the two well-lit sequences VGGT-Long stays close to the ground truth (Fig.~\ref{fig:overlay}), with an ATE of 0.15\,m and 0.26\,m (Tab.~\ref{tab:results}). The median map accuracy is 3.0\,cm and 4.7\,cm, the F-score at 10\,cm is 0.74 and 0.71. The five runs differ by less than 0.1\,mm, so the pipeline is practically deterministic and the IQR says nothing about robustness.
On \emph{Varying-illu01} the ATE rises to 2.35\,m and the map cannot be registered to the reference, although the estimate covers 99.9\,\% of the sequence and the system reports no error. After alignment the trajectory shrinks to a small area near the room centre (scale 2.1 instead of 5.2 to 5.8 on the other sequences). The error is therefore mostly the distance of the robot from the centre. It goes up and down with each lap, not with the dark phases (Fig.~\ref{fig:darkness}).

\section{Summary and Outlook}
% ~0.5 pages. Interpret failure modes in the context of theory, limitations, outlook.
VGGT-Long fails without noticing it. In the dark the network still outputs poses, and these wrong poses go into the chunk alignment and the loop closure. The tracked fraction stays at 99.9\,\% while the trajectory is wrong, so for learned visual SLAM the tracked fraction alone does not show whether a run worked. Replacing VGGT with the metric model MapAnything~\cite{keetha2025mapanything} did not help on our sequences: its scale was off by a factor of 4 to 16 and the ATE grew to 4 to 16\,m.
